TrigScript Showcase #3, Für Elise: the sound probe
=================================================

StarCraft: Remastered, hosted as a multiplayer game, alone, with the game's MUSIC OFF. Headphones help.
Record one run (about 3 min 45 s). It plays five items, each announced in the text lines before it, a few
seconds apart, then says it is done. Each item is one sound mixed when the map was built, so every note is
exactly where it was written.
The five items are also beside this sheet, in fur-elise-sound-preview/, to play outside the game (the same Ogg
files the map has).

The lobby should say "Für Elise sound probe". Does the ü show, or something else? ->

1 of 5 — the old way (32 s): one recording of the piano's middle C, sped up or slowed for every other key, imported
  as the editor imports a sound (one channel, 22 kHz), every note on the grid at one strength, no pedal.
  This is what music.sampler could do before. It is here only to compare with.
  How does it sound to you (a few words)? ->

2 of 5 — the sampled piano, still on the grid (35 s): the Salamander grand, the recording made nearest each key
  (a recording every three keys, moved at most one semitone), the one made at each note's strength (16 strengths),
  the damper's sound when a key is let go. Same notes, same tempo, same strength as item 1, no pedal.
  Better than 1? Does it sound like a real grand piano? ->
  Anything wrong: a note out of tune, a click, a note cut off oddly? ->

3 of 5 — played (49 s): the same opening, but with its own tempo (it leans into bar 4, slows into the middle part,
  holds back on the turn to the high E, slows into the last A), dynamics (p, the middle part rising, pp on the turn,
  the last A ppp), the pedal on each arpeggio as Beethoven marks it (and its sound), the strings ringing with the
  pedal down (sympathetic resonance), and a concert hall.
  Times in the item: 0:13 the middle part (C major), 0:17 the turn (E, the high E, E–D#), 0:23 the theme again,
  0:34 the last A, held in the pedal until about 0:40, then the hall's tail.
  Does it feel played by a person, or still mechanical? ->
  The tempo: too much rubato, too little, or right? ->
  The pedal: is each arpeggio's pedal clean (no blur into the E–D# figure)? ->
  The strings ringing with the pedal: can you hear them? Do they sound like a piano, or like a metallic ring? ->
  The hall: too much, too little, right? (The plan is a smaller room for the first A, this hall for the last.) ->
  Loudness: the piano items are made to −17 LUFS, the storm to −15. Too quiet against the game, fine? ->

4 of 5 — the storm (32 s): the D minor section over the drumming A (C1 … C17), piano and orchestra: violins on the
  chords' top line, violas under them, celli spiccato doubling the drumming bass, basses an octave down, horns and
  trombones on the harmony, tuba on the bass, timpani on A and E. f, a lighter middle (mf), then rising over the
  B-flat and B to the ff A minor chord, and the quiet E chord that ends it.
  Times: 0:00 C1 (the diminished chord), 0:11 C9 (it starts again), 0:15 the climb over B-flat, 0:20 the ff chord.
  Does the orchestra sound like an orchestra? Which instrument sounds worst? ->
  Balance: is the piano still the lead? Too much brass? Timpani? Strings too quiet or too loud? ->
  Is it "stormy" enough, or should it be bigger (faster, louder, more timpani, rolls)? ->

5 of 5 — into the storm (46 s): the theme's last bars as in item 3 (pp), one bar of the drumming A alone getting
  louder under a soft timpani roll, then the storm's first phrase with the orchestra coming in (at 0:12).
  Does the change from the solo piano to the orchestra work? Too sudden, too soft, right? ->

Anything wrong anywhere: crackle, clipping, a sound that cuts off, a hitch in the game? ->

What to decide (your call):
  - The arc: keep the orchestra to the storm only, or let the strings creep in earlier (the last bars before it)? ->
  - Item 3's hall for the last A; a smaller room for the first? ->
  - More velocity layers are in the piano (16); the probe uses all of them. Fine to keep the map at ~3.5 MB? ->

transcription and the arrangement are ours, written from the Breitkopf & Härtel complete edition (1888).
