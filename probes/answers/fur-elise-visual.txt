TrigScript Showcase #3 probe: the Für Elise visualizer
======================================================

Map: trigscript-fur-elise-visual.scx (lobby "Für Elise visual probe"). About 100 seconds, nothing to press.

What it asks: does a piano roll drawn by the game look good on video, and does the picture stay with the music? The
piano is a stand-in (an electric piano); the real piano comes from the sound probe. Every note's time is worked out
when the map is built, and the picture of every frame with it.

What to record
--------------
- Host it as a multiplayer game, alone (the computer slot stays a computer). Remastered graphics, no F5.
- Options: game MUSIC OFF, sound effects ON (the piano is played as sound effects). Widescreen, as you normally play.
- Record the whole run from the lobby to "The end. Thank you." Do not move the camera or click on the map; the camera
  is put back on the stage every 4 seconds anyway.

What is where
-------------
- Minimap: a piano roll on black. Notes fall onto a keyboard row (white keys white, black keys indigo) that lights as
  each key sounds: right hand gold, left hand cyan, the storm's chords red.
- Main screen: the same as Flags: a keyboard of 30 white keys with the black keys above them, notes falling onto it.
- Top left of the main screen: the sync lamp, a start-location ring. Black, and white on each frame a note starts
  (and on each click in part T). This is what the recording is measured by: keep it in view.

The parts (seconds from the start of the game, about; each is named in the text lines as it starts)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
T    2   six clicks, a second apart, the lamp white on each.
A1   9   the A section. Falling notes, a row every frame on the main screen.
A2  22   the same music on: the main screen's notes fall half as fast (a row every other frame).
C-a 45   a stormy passage in D minor: the white keys flash red on each chord, Disruption Webs on the downbeats.
C-b 50   the same storm with explosions on the downbeats (Wraiths, and three Battlecruisers on the last chords).
         The game's own explosion sounds will be heard here.
S1  57   the theme twice, played as one long clip.
S2  72   the same theme, played as a clip a bar, each bar started by the game.
V1  86   the theme again, the notes rising from the keys after they sound.
V2  93   the keys alone, no rolls.
    102  "The end. Thank you."

Questions
---------
1. Did the lobby show the name with the ü ("Für Elise visual probe"), and the first text line too? ->
2. Minimap: do the falling notes read as a piano roll (columns of dots onto the keyboard row)? Too small, too sparse? ->
3. Minimap: can you tell the hands apart (gold / cyan), and the black keys from the white? ->
4. Main screen: do the Flags read as a keyboard, and the falling notes as notes? Which colours get lost? ->
5. Main screen: A1 (a row a frame) or A2 (half as fast): which is better to watch? Does A1 look smooth? ->
6. Does the picture feel in time with the sound everywhere? Anywhere it feels early or late? ->
7. C-a: do the red flashes look good or too much? Did any Disruption Web appear, and did it make a sound? ->
8. C-b: did the explosions appear where the chords are, and are the game's explosion sounds acceptable over the
   music, or should effects be silent? ->
9. S1 and S2 should sound the same. Any stutter, flam or gap in S2 at the bar lines? ->
10. V1 (rising) and V2 (keys alone) against A1 (falling): which look do you want for the Showcase? ->
11. Did the game slow down or stutter anywhere? ->
12. Anything else (a unit in the wrong place, a stray Flag or Overlord, an error text)? ->

What I measure from the recording (no need to fill in)
------------------------------------------------------
- T: each click's start in the audio against the lamp turning white: the plain delay between sound and picture.
- A, S1: each note's start (the audio's attacks) against the lamp's flashes, over the whole clip: the delay and
  whether it grows (drift) over 34 s and 12 s.
- S2 against S1: the same notes, the same lamp. Each bar of S2 starts on the mixer's 21.3 ms grid on its own, so its
  delay may change by up to 21 ms from bar to bar; S1's should stay the same through the clip.
- Minimap redraws: how often the roll moves (expected about 12 times a second).
